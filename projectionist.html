As You Like It (1936)

Directed by: Paul Czinner
Watched: 23 Jun 2026
Rating: ★★★★☆

It's excellent—especially its screenplay. Fully half the play must be cut, and there's no room at all for pause, but at 96 minutes it feels cinematic and cheerful.

Laurence Olivier is basically a god who walks the Forest of Arden. It's ridiculous, it strains credulity, and yet there is some sense in it, for Elisabeth Bergner (an Austrian Jewish refugee, incidentally, along with director Paul Czinner) outmatches him both in beauty and in screen presence (once she embodies Ganymede, certainly—it's a particularly strong Ganymede performance).

What little slack exists is left for the actors playing Jaques and Frederick to chew on.

Whisper of the Heart (1995)

Directed by: Yoshifumi Kondō
Watched: 11 Jun 2026
Rating: ★★★★⯪

So beautiful. In Whisper of the Heart, Shizuku experiences a bit of a breakthrough. Whereas Miyazaki would deliver his signature brand of somno-mythic esoterica, Kondō instead kneads in a deep and gentle socio-economic ecology.

A literal and figurative ecology, where relations between people and non-human animals feel inextricably connected to weather, shelter, sunlight, hunger, mineral genesis, and cross-species mutuality.

The Testament of Ann Lee (2026)

Directed by: Mona Fastvold
Watched: 19 May 2026
Rating: ★★★⯪☆

Shot on 35mm film by the duo behind The Brutalist, one third of The Testament of Ann Lee is indistinguishable from a neoclassical painting or Dutch still life: unnaturally bright skies, white interiors, dark varnished foreground detail, and wide angle lenses mimicking revolutionary, history-making framing.

These moments become deeply personal through Amanda Seyfried's delivery. She's captivating as Lee. I find it interesting how both her performance and the story itself invoke and tie together proto- and contemporary feminist threads, seeking a balance through tradition and liberation that perhaps we ourselves have yet to canonize.

And yet the story felt somewhat shallow to me, with under-written scenes and dialogue, intrusive voiceover, lacklustre lyrics, and an apparent need to rush through the last ten years of Ann Lee's life instead of climaxing on one of the many triumphant moments we only glimpse.
